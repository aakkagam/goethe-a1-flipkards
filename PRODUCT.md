# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Adult self-learners preparing for the Goethe-Zertifikat A1 exam on their own schedule. They study mostly on a phone, in short 5 to 10 minute sessions (commute, breaks), often one-handed. The job: get through the official A1 word list, learn each word's meaning and usage, and know which words they still don't know.

## Product Purpose

Flip cards for the official Goethe A1 vocabulary list. Front: the German word (with article and plural). Back: meaning, a sample sentence, and the English translation of that sentence on request, so learners try to understand the German first. Learners mark each card known or unknown and re-review the unknown ones. Success: a learner opens it, reviews a batch in minutes, and over time sees the unknown pile shrink.

## Brand Personality

Calm, focused, encouraging. A quiet study companion, low pressure. Feels polished and native, in the spirit of Things and Bear: considered details, nothing shouting. Progress is acknowledged plainly, never with guilt or hype.

## Anti-references

- Duolingo-style gamification: mascots, streak guilt, confetti, XP, leaderboards.
- German flag colors (black, red, gold) or other national clichés.
- Generic SaaS template: gradient hero, identical card grids, stat tiles.
- Cluttered textbook: dense tables, tiny type, everything on screen at once.

## Design Principles

1. **One card, full attention.** Each screen centers a single word; everything else recedes.
2. **Thumb first.** Every core action (flip, known, unknown, reveal translation) is reachable one-handed on a phone.
3. **Try before telling.** Translation stays hidden until asked, so the learner engages with the German first.
4. **Quiet progress.** Show what is left and what is known, factually, without pressure or rewards theater.
5. **The list is the authority.** German forms follow the Goethe list exactly; presentation never obscures article and plural.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target. Sensible defaults: readable contrast, legible type sizes on small screens, keyboard support on desktop, and respect for `prefers-reduced-motion` on the card flip.
